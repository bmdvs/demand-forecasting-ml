Section 1

Status & Retraining Decision

The demand forecasting model has been monitored monthly across its live window, January to June 2026. Forecast error and bias are the primary model triggers (i.e., when these exceed their Retrain thresholds, the model must be retrained), while target, prediction and feature drift are leading indicators. Business KPIs including fill rates, stockout days and jobs held are also monitored against target levels.

A forecast can only be scored once its lead-time window has closed, so each month is judged on accuracy once at least 80% of its forecasts have matured. By June 30th that covers January to April; May and June are shown but not yet judged on accuracy.

The flag reads RETRAIN RECOMMENDED, meaning the model should be fully retrained on data through June. This is because, after bias correction, the forecasts for intermittent items ran high in March and April (+27% and +24%), outside the ±10% Retrain threshold: the correction factors, fit on the prior year's errors, over-correct intermittent items. Separately, the safety buffers should be recalibrated, because the fill rate achieved ran more than 1 point below target for a criticality group in January, February, March and May.

Section 2.2

Drift

Drift compares each month's data with a fixed reference calculated using the Jensen-Shannon distance, flagged at 0.10. This calculation checks for statistical similarity: whether this month's values are similar to the reference data (the training rows for usage and features, and the held-out 2025 forecasts for the model's forecasts), where 0 means the two are identical and 1 means they do not overlap at all. It is checked three ways: on the targets (usage over the lead-time window), on the model's forecasts, and on the model's features.

Target drift. Distance between each month's actual usage over the lead-time window and the training rows. Target drift stays well under the threshold (0.026 to 0.054), meaning monthly usage over the lead-time window resembles the usage the model was trained on.

Target Drift Distance by Month

Prediction drift. Distance between each month's forecasts and the forecasts from the held-out 2025 year. Prediction drift stays under the threshold every month (at most 0.054), meaning the model is producing forecasts on the same scale and spread as in 2025.

Feature drift. Per-feature distance between each month's inputs and the training rows, for the 24 features other than the calendar ones, which change with the date by design. Section 2.5

Supplier Lead-Time Review

The review compares each part's pooled 26-week lead time with its corrected master lead time at each month-end, and flags a part when the pooled figure runs more than 25% above the master for 2 month-ends running; suppliers are checked the same way. A flag sends the master to purchasing for review; the reorder point keeps using the master until it is changed. Parts flagged rose from 22 in January to 69 in June, with 2 supplier flags over the six months.
